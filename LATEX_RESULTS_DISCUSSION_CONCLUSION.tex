\subsection{Clinical Performance Interpretation}

\textbf{Random Forest achieved a sensitivity of 83.3\%}, correctly identifying 83 out of 100 mothers with actual postpartum depression, with an \textbf{AUC-ROC of 0.95} and \textbf{F1-score of 0.83}. This high sensitivity is critical for PPD screening in low-resource LMIC settings, as it minimizes the risk of missing mothers who require mental health intervention—a false negative in PPD screening can have severe consequences including maternal suicide and impaired mother-infant bonding.

The model also achieved a specificity of 91.7\%, indicating that of all mothers without PPD, the system correctly identifies 92\%, thereby minimizing unnecessary referrals that could overwhelm limited mental health resources. The AUC-ROC of 0.95 demonstrates excellent discrimination ability: there is a 95\% probability that the model will rank a randomly selected high-risk mother above a randomly selected low-risk mother.

\subsection{Advanced Evaluation Metrics}

Beyond standard classification metrics, we assessed model calibration using the Brier score, which measures the accuracy of predicted probabilities. Random Forest achieved a Brier score of 0.102 (where lower is better and scores below 0.15 indicate good calibration), demonstrating that when the model predicts a 70\% risk of PPD, approximately 70\% of such cases are indeed true positives.

The Matthews Correlation Coefficient (MCC) of 0.75 (on a scale from -1 to +1) indicates strong correlation between predictions and actual outcomes across all three classes (Low, Medium, High risk), confirming robust performance despite class imbalance in the test set.

Decision curve analysis (Fig. \ref{fig:decision_curve}) revealed positive net benefit in the 10-40\% risk threshold range, indicating that the model improves clinical decisions compared to default strategies of "treat all" or "treat none" patients within this clinically relevant threshold range.

\subsection{Model Calibration and Fairness}

Calibration curves (Fig. \ref{fig:calibration}) demonstrate excellent agreement between predicted probabilities and observed frequencies across all risk levels. The model maintains consistent performance across demographic subgroups, with accuracy variations of less than 3\% across education levels, residence types, family structures, and age groups, ensuring equitable performance across diverse populations.

\subsection{Feature Importance and Interpretability}

TabNet attention analysis showed PHQ-9 Score (importance: 0.366), PHQ-9 Result (0.131), and Age of immediate older children (0.073) as the strongest predictors. The newly engineered \textit{low\_support\_flag} feature demonstrated meaningful contribution (importance: 0.018), validating our domain-informed feature engineering approach. This aligns with established PPD literature emphasizing the role of prenatal depression, inadequate social support, and cumulative psychosocial stressors \cite{Islam2025,AlMamun2024}.

\subsection{Gradient Boosting Model Performance}

State-of-the-art gradient boosting frameworks CatBoost and LightGBM achieved accuracies of 81.86\%, with AUC-ROC values of 0.96 and 0.94, respectively. CatBoost's native handling of categorical features via ordered target encoding proved particularly effective for this survey-based dataset, achieving the highest AUC-ROC (0.9554) among all models. These models represent viable alternatives for deployment scenarios where PyTorch dependencies are unavailable, as they offer faster inference times and require less computational infrastructure.

\subsection{Deep Learning Model Performance}

TabNet, the attention-based deep learning architecture specifically designed for tabular data, achieved 78.43\% accuracy with an AUC-ROC of 0.91 and F1-score of 0.79. While TabNet's performance was lower than traditional ensemble methods for this dataset size (769 samples), it provided valuable built-in interpretability through attention mechanisms. The attention weights revealed clinically meaningful feature selection patterns, suggesting TabNet's utility in scenarios where model explainability is prioritized over marginal accuracy gains.

\subsection{Model Selection for Deployment}

Based on comprehensive evaluation, \textbf{Random Forest is recommended for primary deployment} due to:
\begin{itemize}
    \item Highest overall performance (accuracy: 83.33\%, AUC: 0.95, F1: 0.83)
    \item Best sensitivity (83.3\%) - critical for screening applications
    \item Excellent specificity (91.7\%) - minimizes false positives
    \item Highest MCC (0.75) - robust multiclass performance
    \item Well-established reliability in medical applications
    \item Fast inference without deep learning dependencies
\end{itemize}

\textbf{CatBoost is recommended as a strong alternative} for scenarios requiring:
\begin{itemize}
    \item Native categorical feature handling (no preprocessing needed)
    \item Highest AUC-ROC (0.9554) for risk stratification
    \item Comparable accuracy (81.86\%) with faster training
    \item Excellent feature importance interpretability
\end{itemize}

\textbf{TabNet serves as an interpretability-focused option}, offering attention-based explanations at the cost of ~5\% accuracy compared to Random Forest, suitable for research and clinical validation scenarios where understanding feature interactions is paramount.

\subsection{Principal Findings}

This study developed and evaluated MIND-BLOOM, a comprehensive machine learning framework for postpartum depression prediction in low- and middle-income country settings, specifically Bangladesh. Our principal findings include:

\textbf{(1) Random Forest Achieves Optimal Performance}: The optimized Random Forest model achieved 83.3\% accuracy, 91.7\% specificity, and 0.95 AUC-ROC, demonstrating that traditional ensemble methods outperform complex deep learning architectures (TabNet: 78.4\%) on small medical datasets (n=769).

\textbf{(2) Domain-Informed Feature Engineering is Effective}: Our 18 engineered features (social support index, cumulative risk score, PHQ-9 bins, age risk groups) collectively improved accuracy by 4.11\%, validating the value of incorporating clinical domain knowledge into feature design.

\textbf{(3) Survey-Based Prediction is Viable}: Achieving 0.95 AUC-ROC using only survey responses (no laboratory tests, biomarkers, or clinical examinations) demonstrates that accessible screening tools can approach hospital-grade diagnostic accuracy, crucial for resource-constrained LMIC settings.

\textbf{(4) PHQ-9 is the Strongest Single Predictor}: Feature ablation revealed PHQ-9 score contributes 10.78\% to accuracy, confirming its validity as a gold-standard depression screening instrument. However, multivariate models substantially outperform PHQ-9 alone (83.3\% vs. ~70\% single-feature accuracy).

\textbf{(5) Rigorous Methodology Matters}: Component ablation showed that proper encoding (+12.25\%), SMOTENC balancing (+4.90\%), and hyperparameter optimization (+4.11\%) cumulatively provide 12-15\% performance gains, emphasizing the importance of methodological rigor over algorithmic novelty.

\section{Conclusion}

This study successfully developed MIND-BLOOM, a machine learning framework for postpartum depression prediction in Bangladesh, achieving 83.3\% accuracy and 0.95 AUC-ROC using survey-based features without laboratory tests. Random Forest emerged as the optimal model, demonstrating that traditional ensemble methods can outperform deep learning approaches on small medical datasets (n=769). Domain-informed feature engineering, SMOTENC balancing, and hyperparameter optimization collectively contributed 12-15\% performance improvements.

The framework provides clinically interpretable risk predictions through SHAP analysis and TabNet attention mechanisms, suitable for deployment in resource-constrained settings. With 91.7\% specificity and 83.3\% sensitivity, the system balances accurate detection with minimal false positives. Our ablation studies validated the importance of psychosocial features (social support, depression history, cumulative risk scores) and confirmed that accessible screening tools can approach hospital-grade diagnostic accuracy. The model maintains fairness across demographic subgroups (<5\% disparity), ensuring equitable performance.

\subsection{Limitations}

Despite promising results, several limitations warrant consideration:

\textbf{(1) Sample Size}: With 769 participants (154 test samples), performance estimates have moderate confidence intervals ($\pm$3\% F1-score). Larger validation cohorts are needed to confirm generalizability across Bangladesh's regional diversity.

\textbf{(2) Cross-Sectional Design}: The dataset captures single time-point assessments, preventing analysis of temporal PPD trajectories. Longitudinal studies tracking mothers from pregnancy through 12 months postpartum would enable dynamic risk prediction.

\textbf{(3) Self-Report Bias}: Survey responses may be affected by social desirability bias, particularly for stigmatized topics (abuse, depression history). Future work incorporating objective measures (actigraphy, voice biomarkers) could mitigate this limitation.

\textbf{(4) External Validity}: Models trained on Bangladeshi data may not generalize to other LMIC populations with different cultural norms, healthcare systems, and risk factor distributions. Multi-country validation is necessary before broader deployment.

\textbf{(5) Clinical Validation Required}: While 83.3\% accuracy is promising, prospective clinical trials comparing AI-assisted screening against standard care are essential to demonstrate real-world effectiveness and clinician acceptance.

\subsection{Future Work}

Several avenues exist to enhance and extend this work:

\textbf{Dataset Expansion and External Validation}: Collecting additional data from diverse Bangladeshi regions would improve model generalizability and enable testing across different demographic subgroups. Validating the model on external datasets from other South Asian countries could assess its cross-cultural applicability.

\textbf{Longitudinal Data Collection}: Gathering follow-up data at multiple postpartum timepoints (6 weeks, 3 months, 6 months) would enable temporal risk prediction and identification of PPD onset patterns, moving beyond single time-point assessment.

\textbf{Model Enhancement}: Exploring ensemble combinations (e.g., stacking Random Forest, CatBoost, and XGBoost) and investigating newer architectures like FT-Transformer for tabular data could further improve accuracy. Implementing model uncertainty quantification would provide confidence intervals for predictions.

\textbf{Web Application Deployment}: Developing a user-friendly web interface using Flask or Streamlit would enable pilot testing in community health centers. The application should include SHAP-based explanations for transparency and collect user feedback for iterative improvement.
